2. **`Jaisy_Portfolio_.pdf`** (the older company portfolio) — kept as **Section B** further
   down in this file. Use it to *fill in depth the repositioning plan doesn't specify*:
   - the longer bullet lists under each service (e.g. all 8 signage sub-offerings)
   - the full 39-name client list and the portfolio image inventory (§B.9–B.10)
   - the complete 11-item industries list and full contact/address details
   - extra "Why Jaisy" context and the Vision/Mission statements (the repositioning plan
     doesn't include a separate Vision/Mission slide — keep them from the portfolio as
     supporting About-page content)
3. **When the two disagree on wording, structure, or which items to feature first, Section
   A (the repositioning plan) wins** — e.g. its 6-service naming/order, its 4-card "Why
   Brands Choose Jaisy," and its 10-item industries list are what should headline the site.
   Section B's longer versions of the same lists can still be used as expandable/secondary
   detail (e.g. expand a service card to reveal its full portfolio bullet list), but the
   short, business-approved copy is what renders by default.
4. **Design reference**: the repositioning-plan deck itself was built with **Stavok
   Grotesque** (bold display headings) and **Open Sauce One** (body/UI text) — see
   PROMPT.md §1 for how to apply this.

  PROMPT.md §1.

  highly legible — matches the "body copy" font direction already specified in PROMPT.md.
- A small amount of "Arimo" also appears (likely an incidental fallback within the Canva
  file, not an intentional brand choice) — ignore it as a design reference.
- **Action for the build agent:** use "Open Sauce One" directly (it's a free, licensed
  font available via Fontshare — https://www.fontshare.com/fonts/open-sauce-one — and can
  be self-hosted or loaded via `next/font/local`). For "Stavok Grotesque," check licensing
  before use; if it isn't freely licensed for web embedding, substitute a close free
  alternative with the same bold/geometric character (e.g. "Space Grotesk," "General
  Sans," or "Archivo Black" at heavy weight) rather than blocking the build on font
  procurement.
